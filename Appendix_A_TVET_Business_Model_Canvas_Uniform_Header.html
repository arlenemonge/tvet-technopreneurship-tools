TVET Technopreneurship Business Model Canvas

From technical skills and innovation to a potential enterprise.

Introduction

The Business Model Canvas (BMC) is a strategic planning tool that helps entrepreneurs organize the essential components of a business idea. It outlines how a venture creates value, delivers products or services, generates revenue, and manages resources, activities, partnerships, and costs.

In technopreneurship, the canvas connects technical expertise and technological innovation with customer needs and business viability. It makes the pathway from TVET learning to enterprise creation explicit: identify competencies gained through practical training, apply them and relevant technologies to a real customer or industry need, and shape the solution into a potentially viable venture. Where applicable, link competencies to the relevant curriculum, qualification, or competency standards. A business is not TVET-related merely because it uses digital platforms; its applied competencies and practical outputs should be clear.

Use it for technical services, digital solutions, innovative products, improved production processes, or other technology-enabled ventures. A completed canvas is an initial plan, not proof of commercial viability.

How to use it

  1. Enter a venture name and TVET/technical field, including relevant competencies or qualification where applicable.
  2. Complete each of the nine components with concise notes.
  3. Use customer feedback, industry insights, or preliminary research where possible.
  4. Review assumptions and revise the canvas as you learn.
  5. Use Appendix B to validate your idea before committing significant resources.

Illustrative TVET Example: Digital Marketing and E-Commerce Venture

Problem: Small businesses may struggle to present their products online and consistently reach potential customers.

TVET competency connection: Applied competencies may include digital content production, graphic design, e-commerce operations, digital customer service, and basic data analytics. These should be aligned with the relevant training or competency standards where applicable.

Practical outputs: Build or improve an online storefront, prepare product photographs and listings, create campaign content, respond to customer inquiries, and produce a simple performance report with recommendations.

Solution: A digital service that helps small businesses set up or improve an online storefront, create product content, manage social media campaigns, and use basic performance metrics to improve their online presence.

Resources: Demonstrable digital content, e-commerce, customer service, and analytics competencies; computer or smartphone; internet access; design and analytics tools; a portfolio; and relevant training or competency evidence.

Potential income: Monthly service packages, one-time online-store setup fees, content-creation fees, and campaign-management services.

Illustrative only. Feasibility depends on customer demand, competition, platform costs, privacy and data-protection requirements, and the ability to deliver measurable value.
